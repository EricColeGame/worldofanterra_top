## How to Spot a Fake or Speculative Release Date

Release date misinformation is rampant for anticipated indies. The pattern is predictable: an aggregator scrapes a placeholder date, a content mill turns it into a headline, and suddenly a "confirmed" launch day is circulating that the developer never announced.

Use this checklist before you believe anything:

| Claim you encounter | How to verify it | Red flag |
|---|---|---|
| "Confirmed for [specific date]" | Check the Steam listing banner and news hub | No link back to Steam or the developer |
| "Leaked store backend date" | Ignore unless a major outlet reports it with a source | Screenshot-only "evidence" |
| "Dev said Q4 in an interview" | Find the original interview and read the quote | Quote is paraphrased with no link |
| "Pre-orders are live" | Look for a purchase button on Steam | Links to a reseller, not Valve |
| "Delayed to next year" | Check the news hub for an official post | Article cites "sources close to the project" |

Player experience in similar indie launches suggests a simple rule: if the claim doesn't trace back to the Steam page or a first-party channel, file it as a rumor and move on. You lose nothing by waiting — the wishlist email will reach you either way.
