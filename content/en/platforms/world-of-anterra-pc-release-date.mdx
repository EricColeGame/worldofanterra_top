## Why the Date Hasn't Been Announced Yet

The Steam description gives a fairly clear picture of why this project is taking time. The game is described as **100% human made**, with hand-animated sprites, a custom animation layering system, and a bespoke real-time lighting engine. Those are the kinds of features that scale with hours rather than with tooling.

Consider what has to be finished and tested before a date can be responsibly announced:

- **Non-linear story and quests.** Branching content multiplies the number of states that need QA passes.
- **Deep progression systems.** Party builds, gear combinations, and crafted items all interact, which invites balance work late in development.
- **Hybrid combat.** Blending quick-time actions with grid-based strategy means every enemy encounter needs both timing and tactical tuning.
- **Hand-animated art.** Every piece of gear that appears on a party member needs to layer correctly across the animation set.
- **A living world.** Dynamic weather, day-night cycles, water shaders, and special effects all need to hold up across the entire map.

None of that is a delay — it is just the natural cost of handcrafting an open world. Studios that ship this kind of game typically announce a date only when content is locked and the remaining work is polish and certification. Until then, silence is normal.

### How to Spot a Fake or Placeholder Date

Community reports and player experience with other Steam indies point to the same handful of traps. Watch for these:

- Retail or aggregator listings showing a neat date like "December 31" — that is a placeholder convention, not a plan.
- YouTube thumbnails and blog headlines that state a date the store page does not.
- "Leaked" dates with no link back to the developer or Steam.
- Regional store pages showing a different date than the main listing.

If a date cannot be traced to the Steam page or a direct developer statement, treat it as unverified.
