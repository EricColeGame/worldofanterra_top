## What Actually Happens Inside the Server

The structure of the community is built around three recurring rhythms. Understanding them is the fastest way to go from confused newcomer to comfortable regular.

### Monthly Developer Fireside Chats

This is the headline event. Once a month, the team holds a fireside-style conversation with the community — the kind of format that rewards people who show up with real questions rather than people who just want a hype reel. The practical advice here is to prepare. Read the previous chat's summary if one exists, check whether your question has already been answered in a pinned message, and then ask something specific. "What's the status of X?" gets a vague answer. "How did the art direction for X change after the last round of feedback?" gets a real one.

If you cannot attend live, do not panic. Community reports suggest that the most useful parts of these sessions — design philosophy, art direction, musical influences — tend to surface in discussion threads afterward, which is why reading the backlog is worth the effort.

### Art, Design, and Music Updates

The second rhythm is the steady drip of development material: concept art, design decisions, and music. For a role-playing game, this is where worldbuilding becomes tangible. Pay attention to music posts in particular — soundtracks are often the last thing a small team finalizes, so early music shares are a genuine signal that production is moving.

### Everyday Conversation

The third rhythm is just people talking. Theorycrafting, lore speculation, build ideas, "what class would you play" threads — the ordinary texture of a game community. The team describes the crowd as kind and like-minded, and in practice that usually means the moderation culture rewards curiosity over gatekeeping.

| Content Type | Rough Cadence | Best Way to Consume It |
|---|---|---|
| Developer Fireside chat | Monthly | Attend live or read the recap thread |
| Art previews | Regular, as work is finished | Follow the art channel |
| Design discussion | Regular, tied to development milestones | Search for the topic before asking |
| Music shares | Occasional, when tracks are ready | Check pinned posts in the audio channel |
| Player chatter | Constant | Dip in; no need to keep up |

## Etiquette: How to Be the Kind of Member People Are Glad to See

Every server has a culture, and cultures are shaped by small behaviors. These are the habits that separate a valued community member from a name people scroll past.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Read the rules and pinned messages first | Ask a question that is pinned at the top of the channel |
| Use the search bar before starting a thread | Create a new thread for a topic that already has one |
| Tag spoilers, especially lore and story details | Drop plot details into general chat without warning |
| Give specific, constructive feedback | Post "this looks bad" with no reasoning |
| Keep self-promotion to designated channels | DM developers with unsolicited pitches |
| Stay kind when you disagree | Turn a design debate into a personal argument |

One more habit that pays off: when a developer shares work in progress, respond to the thing they actually made. Critique the lighting, the palette, the instrumentation — specifics invite dialogue. Generic praise and generic complaints both get the same shrug.

No. The World of Anterra Discord is a community space for people following the project's development, including those who have not played anything yet. Reading the pinned channels is the fastest way to get oriented.
